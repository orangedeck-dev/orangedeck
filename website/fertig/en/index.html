What it is

OrangeDeck is a Bitcoin mempool visualizer. It draws the mempool the way it actually behaves: every waiting transaction is a square. It piles up, it gets mined, it gets replaced, and you see it happen instead of reading a number.

Around that sit four more views: a block height clock for a tablet on the wall, mining figures from your own Bitaxe and from the network, a block explorer, and a market tab with candles, cumulative volume delta and liquidations.

Five views

Feed

The mempool as a living mosaic, with the last block beside it. Colour by age, fee or transaction type.

Clock

Block height, fees, price, hashrate and the halving countdown, large and without controls. For a tablet on the wall.

Mining

A Bitaxe monitor: hashrate, best share against the network difficulty, temperature and power of your own miner. Beside it the network with hashrate, difficulty and the next adjustment.

Explorer

Search by height, hash, txid or address. Browse blocks and their contents, see what replaced what.

Market

Price as candles or a line, volume, cumulative volume delta and liquidations, aggregated across exchanges.

Set up the way you want it

Every tab can be switched off, and the ones that stay can be put in any order. Inside each view you decide which parts are shown. OrangeDeck shows only what you want to see.

  • Only your tabsSwitch off any tab you do not need, change the order and pick the view it opens with.
  • Switching on its ownTabs can change by themselves every 30 seconds to 10 minutes, and you choose which ones take part.
  • Every view in partsHeader, footer, block details, legend, charts and bars can each be turned on or off. The clock lets you choose what stands large and whether values take turns.
  • Tiles your wayColour the tiles by age, fee or type, size them by value or by vBytes, and make them larger or smaller.
  • The market as you read itCandles or line, volume or CVD, time range, crosshair and trade tape, each one a setting.
  • Language, currency, sourceThirteen languages, seven currencies and any mempool instance, your own node included.

Where the numbers come from

Blocks, mempool, fees, hashrate
The public WebSocket of mempool.space, another public mempool instance or your own node. That is one field in the settings.
Price and trades
The public streams of Binance, Bybit and OKX.
Liquidations
Bybit and OKX. The heatmap is a model built from Binance open interest, and it says so on screen.
Your own miner
The local API of the device, AxeOS or cgminer. The request stays in your home network.

Why OrangeDeck

  1. 01

    It is a viewer, and nothing else

    It knows no private key, cannot sign, and carries no library that could. It only shows.

  2. 02

    No server of ours

    It talks to mempool.space or to the instance you choose, your own node included. There is no counting, no crash reports and no account.

  3. 03

    Measured, not guessed

    Where a number is a model rather than a measurement, as with the liquidation heatmap, it says so on screen, with the assumptions written out.

  4. 04

    One codebase, five hosts

    The same QML draws the window, the widget, the panel and the phone. What is fixed in one place is fixed everywhere.

  5. 05

    Yours to read

    MIT licensed, no obfuscation, and the reasoning is in the commit messages rather than lost.

Questions

Does it need my keys?

No. It holds no keys, cannot sign anything and asks for none of your addresses.

Can I hide the tabs I do not use?

Yes. Every tab can be switched off in the settings and the others can be reordered. The same goes for the parts inside each view, down to single charts and bars.

Where does the data come from?

From mempool.space by default. In the settings you can enter another public mempool instance or your own node; it then applies everywhere, including the widgets and the service. Market data comes from the exchanges' public feeds.

Does it phone home?

No. There is no server of ours, no analytics and no crash reporting.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It is MIT licensed and there is no paid tier.

Which languages does it speak?

Thirteen: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese (Portugal and Brazil), Dutch, Russian, Japanese, Chinese, Polish and Czech. It starts in the system language, and numbers and dates follow the language you pick in the settings.

Is the liquidation heatmap real data?

No, and it says so on screen. Nobody publishes other people's positions; it is a model with its assumptions written out.

Can I run it on a tablet on the wall?

That is what the clock view is for: large figures, no controls. The Android build carries a shortcut straight into it.
