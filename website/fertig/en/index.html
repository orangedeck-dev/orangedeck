What it is

OrangeDeck is a Bitcoin mempool visualizer. It draws the mempool the way it actually behaves: every waiting transaction is a square. It piles up, it gets mined, it gets replaced, and you see it happen instead of reading a number.

Around that sit five more views: a block height clock for a tablet on the wall, mining figures from your own Bitaxe and from the network, a block explorer, a market tab with candles, cumulative volume delta and liquidations, and watch-only addresses.

Six views

  • 01FeedThe mempool as a living mosaic, with the last block beside it. Colour by age, fee or transaction type.
  • 02ClockBlock height, fees, price, hashrate and the halving countdown, large and without controls. For a tablet on the wall.
  • 03MiningA Bitaxe monitor: hashrate, best share against the network difficulty, temperature and power of your own miner. Beside it the network with hashrate, difficulty and the next adjustment.
  • 04ExplorerSearch by height, hash, txid or address. Browse blocks and their contents, see what replaced what.
  • 05MarketPrice as candles or a line, volume, cumulative volume delta and liquidations, aggregated across exchanges.
  • 06WalletYour own addresses or an xpub, watch-only. Your transactions are highlighted while they sit in the heap. Needs the OrangeDeck service on a Linux computer; phones and Windows reach it over the home network.

Where it runs

  • Any Linux desktopAn ordinary window. Wayland with an X11 fallback.
  • As a widget or barLayer-shell on niri, sway, Hyprland, river, labwc.
  • Inside DankMaterialShellListed in the DMS plugin directory. As a dashboard tab, a bar pill, a control center tile or a desktop widget.
  • WindowsA ZIP, unzip and run. The same views; the wallet comes from a service on a Linux computer in the same network.
  • macOSBuilds, but has not been tested yet. There is no package so far.
  • AndroidPhone or tablet. Each view has its own home screen shortcut.

Where the numbers come from

  • Blocks, mempool, fees, hashrateThe public WebSocket of mempool.space, or your own mempool instance. That is one setting.
  • Price and tradesThe public streams of Binance, Bybit and OKX.
  • LiquidationsBybit and OKX. The heatmap is a model built from Binance open interest, and it says so on screen.
  • Your own minerThe local API of the device, AxeOS or cgminer. The request stays in your home network.
  • Watched addressesBalance queries to the same mempool instance. No keys, and nothing else leaves the device.

Why OrangeDeck

  • 01It is a viewer, and nothing elseIt knows no private key, cannot sign, and carries no library that could. Watch-only means watch-only.
  • 02No server of oursIt talks to mempool.space or to your own node. There is no counting, no crash reports and no account.
  • 03Measured, not guessedWhere a number is a model rather than a measurement, as with the liquidation heatmap, it says so on screen, with the assumptions written out.
  • 04One codebase, five hostsThe same QML draws the window, the widget, the panel and the phone. What is fixed in one place is fixed everywhere.
  • 05Yours to readMIT licensed, no obfuscation, and the reasoning is in the commit messages rather than lost.

Questions

Does it need my keys?

No. It cannot sign anything. Watched addresses are entered as addresses or an xpub and never leave your machine except as a balance query.

Where does the data come from?

From mempool.space by default, or from your own mempool instance. That is a single setting. Market data comes from the exchanges' public feeds.

Does it phone home?

No. There is no server of ours, no analytics and no crash reporting.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It is MIT licensed and there is no paid tier.

Which languages does it speak?

Thirteen: English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese (Portugal and Brazil), Dutch, Russian, Japanese, Chinese, Polish and Czech. It starts in the system language, and numbers and dates follow the language you pick in the settings.

Is the liquidation heatmap real data?

No, and it says so on screen. Nobody publishes other people's positions; it is a model with its assumptions written out.

Can I run it on a tablet on the wall?

That is what the clock view is for: large figures, no controls. The Android build carries a shortcut straight into it.
